Speed colours on saved routes · 30 October 2026

Implemented and checked on an emulator

Saved trip and replay maps colour the route by speed, from dark (slow) to yellow (fast). The scale uses the speed range of each trip, and a legend on the map shows the speeds at the two ends. The rules are in SpeedColours.kt, the drawing and the legend in RouteMap.kt, and the decision in ADR 0005.

PartBehaviour
ScaleThe 5th and the 95th percentile of the moving speeds (0.5 m/s or more) of the trip, so a bike ride and a drive both use all colours and a few wrong fixes do not set the ends. The range is at least 2 m/s, centred on the speeds, so a trip at an even speed shows the middle colours.
ColoursThe viridis scale (SpeedRamp in Tokens.kt). Its lightness rises in order, so the order also shows without colour vision. The colours stay the same in both themes and with every colour choice. An outline under the line keeps both ends visible: dark on a light map, white on a dark or satellite map.
DataThe usable GPS speed, averaged over 5 seconds. A part without a usable speed is grey. Network positions stay dashed in the warning colour. A replay mutes the part after the playhead, as before.
LegendThe slow and the fast speed in the chosen units, with seven colour steps between them. A screen reader reads one sentence, for example: dark is 4.5 mph or slower, yellow is 67.1 mph or faster. The legend stays clear of the back button and the map buttons.
ChoiceSpeed colours are the default. The map type menu of a saved trip and Settings > Maps > Route colour on saved trips change between speed colours and one colour. The current trip map always uses one colour.
DrawingOne line for each recorded piece, with map style spans: one span for each run of segments with the same rounded colour (32 steps). The Maps SDK fails when a line with spans gets points while it has no span, so a part without a segment is not drawn.
 
SpeedColours.kt · scale
val slow = moving[((moving.size - 1) * 0.05).roundToInt()]
val fast = moving[((moving.size - 1) * 0.95).roundToInt()]
if (fast - slow >= MIN_RANGE_METERS_PER_SECOND) return SpeedScale(slow, fast)

Trip replay, step 1 · 28 October 2026

Implemented and checked on an emulator

The owner chose concept D, "Player with charts": a map player at a glance, with three linked charts in detail. See ADR 0004. This is step 1: the calculations and the glance view. The detail view with the three charts (step 2) and the wide-screen and largest-text layouts (step 3) are not built yet.

PartBehaviour
CalculationsTripReplay.kt, plain Kotlin, run once off the main thread and kept while the app shows the trip. The replay clock joins the segments without their pauses. At the playhead, the values come from the newest fix for at most 10 seconds, so an old speed never shows as current. A position is interpolated only inside one route line. Barometric height: the standard atmosphere for the shape, the median difference to GPS heights with a vertical accuracy of 10 m or better for the level, 1 second means smoothed over 3 seconds. Plane trips use the GPS height only. Along-track acceleration: each accelerometer reading turned by the game rotation vector, gravity removed, 1 second means, turned to east and north by the angle that best fits the GPS change of velocity in 30 second windows, then projected on the GPS direction of travel. Without the sensor record, it comes from the change of GPS speed.
MomentsStrongest acceleration and braking (at least 0.5 m/s²), fastest speed (the usable maximum), and highest point (when the height changes by 1 m or more). Numbers in time order, the same on the map, the profile, and the chips.
Glance viewMap with the route, numbered moments, and the position at the playhead (a ring for a network position, nothing in a GPS gap). The follow button follows the playhead. Values: replay time, speed or the reason that it is unavailable, height above the chosen reference, and acceleration. A speed profile that is also the scrubber: hatched GPS gaps, a dashed warning line for approximate positions, segment marks, and moment numbers. Previous and Next moment, Play or Pause (the one primary button), and 1×, 4×, 16×, or 64×. Moment chips move the playhead and pause.
RulesThe playhead moves at most 10 times each second, only while the replay plays and the screen is in front. The playhead, play state, and speed stay after a rotation. TalkBack reads the profile as one slider with the time, the speed, and Next and Previous moment actions. Landscape and wide windows use the map with a side pane.

Trip data collection · 28 October 2026

Implemented and checked on an emulator

A trip now collects the data for a later playback and analysis, for example the highest point, the fastest speed, and the strongest acceleration. The app does not show the new data yet. The Speed tab and a recording trip still share one GPS request, which uses the strictest need of the active features. See ADR 0003 and the new terms in GLOSSARY.md.

PartBehaviour
Sensor recordWhile a trip records, AndroidMotionSource stores the accelerometer (m/s², with gravity, along the phone's axes) and the game rotation vector at 50 readings each second, and the barometer (hPa) at up to 10 each second. Events arrive on a worker thread. About every 2 seconds, one chunk for each sensor goes through the recorder's command queue into the sensor_chunks table, in the segment that was open at its time. Pause, Stop, and an interruption stop the sensors; the last readings arrive after them. A sensor that the phone does not have is not recorded.
Network positionsAfter 10 seconds without a GPS fix, a recording trip asks Android's network provider for a position every 5 seconds, until the next GPS fix. The Speed tab never uses them. A network position stores its position and accuracy only: no speed, direction, or height, also when Android supplies them. It forms its own route line. The change between sources adds no distance. The current trip shows No GPS: approximate position, and the map draws the line dashed in the warning colour. A saved trip explains the dashed line in its technical details. GPX files mark these points with <src>network</src>. On by default; Settings can turn it off.
High accuracySettings > Trip recording > High accuracy asks for a fix every 100 ms with no minimum interval and no grouped delivery. On Android 12 and later, a GNSS measurement request with full tracking turns off the receiver's duty cycling. Standard keeps one fix each second. The choice applies to the next trip that starts or resumes, and each segment stores it.
More fix dataEach stored fix has its source (GPS or network) and the number of satellites in the fix, from the GNSS provider's satellites extra.
Activity estimateThe evidence window is now 30 seconds of time. Before, it also stopped at 60 fixes, so 10 fixes each second would never give an estimate.
StorageDatabase version 4 deletes older trips. The app is not published, so it has no migration. The old distance migration and its device test are removed.

  1. Repeat release lint and verify the final release build. The unsigned release build passed on 27 October 2026, but it was not installed or run.
  2. Test GPS outdoors on physical devices. Include stationary use, movement, weak signal, and signal recovery.
  3. Compare the home ring and the sky map with a known direction on physical devices. Check compass calibration, magnetic interference, and power use with the ring on home.
  4. Measure battery use against an idle baseline. Measure cold start and frame timing in a release build.
  5. Test TalkBack, denied and approximate permission, permission revocation, and screen lock. Extend coverage to the minimum supported API and current devices.
  6. Check a physical foldable, closed and open, including fold and unfold during a trip. Check the Android 17 metric card and an Android 16 Live Update on physical phones.
  7. Select the final application ID and launcher artwork. Configure release signing outside Git.
  8. Complete the privacy and store requirements for the final feature set. Verify the signed release.
  9. Create a release Maps API key with billing in the Google Cloud project. Restrict it to the Maps SDK for Android, the final application ID, and the upload and Play App Signing certificates. Do not use a map ID. Declare the Maps SDK data in the Data safety form and the privacy policy.

The current application ID is com.example.cvkegps. The release build is unsigned.

Required · Tracking field validation

Measure power and route quality

  1. Test each activity on physical devices. Include a second phone manufacturer.
  2. Measure battery use, route error, stop drift, missing time, and save delay.
  3. Test Doze, Battery Saver, low storage, queue overflow, and map-network failure.
  4. Check API 29 and API 37 device flows and TalkBack.
  5. Use measured results before selecting fused location, batching, or activity-specific intervals.

Core recording and online route review are implemented. Simulated GPS tests do not prove accuracy or power use.

Planned · iOS app

Keep the product consistent across platforms

  • Agree common measurement units, freshness rules, and trip data fields.
  • Create the native iOS app with its own location and permission handling.
  • Use the iOS design language for its interface.
  • Validate equivalent user flows on iOS devices. Review code sharing only when both apps need it.

No iOS project exists in this repository yet.

Optional · Select after the core works

Additional capabilities

  • Metric and imperial unit selection.
  • GPX import, or GPX altitude converted to height above sea level. GPX export and sharing are implemented.
  • A route line coloured by speed (the Maps SDK supports gradient line spans). It needs a legend and colours that do not carry meaning alone.
  • Update Kotlin to 2.4, then maps-compose to 9.x and Play services Maps to 20.1.
  • Automatic pause and improved activity classification, after field validation.
  • Volume-button controls and a larger speed display mode.

Each addition needs a clear user benefit and a review of battery use, privacy, and test scope.
